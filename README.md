# 🩴 Flying Chanclas — Team Hub

> *"Changing the ALAC World one Chancla at a time."*

---

## What Is This?

**Flying Chanclas** was the internal team hub I designed and built from scratch for my Apple support team — entirely self-taught, with no prior training in the platform or web architecture.

The name is a nod to our team's cultural identity. In Latin households, the *chancla* (sandal) is legendary — thrown with surgical precision by mothers across generations as the ultimate disciplinary tool. Our team wore that humor proudly. We were the Flying Chanclas. ✊🌮

Built on **Apple Chorus** (Apple's internal collaboration platform) in **May 2024**, this multi-page hub served as the single destination for everything our team needed — from celebrating each other to tackling the hardest technical cases in the building.

## 🔑 Why This Matters

- **Built entirely from scratch** — No template. No starter kit. No training. Every single page, section, block, space, and visual break was constructed manually — by hand — from a blank canvas.
- **16 pages total** — A full multi-page site with distinct purposes, audiences, and content across every page.
- **Every link was hand-built** — Navigation, cross-page references, external resource links, WOW recognition links — all researched, created, and placed intentionally.
- **Every image was sourced and placed** — Visual design decisions made by instinct, not instruction. Culturally resonant photography selected deliberately for each section — Lotería cards, La Polar ice cream vendor, birthday cakes, seasonal food.
- **Bilingual copywriting throughout** — Page titles, captions, and section names written in both English and Spanish. Culture was baked into the product, not added as an afterthought. *"No manches." "Que hiciste?" "Y tú que piensas?"*
- **Original team branding** — Invented the "Chancludos" naming convention for seasonal events — a creative mashup that extended the team's cultural identity into new content.
- **Every layout element was crafted** — Spacing, content blocks, image cards, section dividers, visual hierarchy — all of it deliberate, all of it self-directed.
- **Full architecture** — Designed the entire multi-page site structure and navigation from the ground up.
- **Used by real people** — This wasn't a demo. My team used it daily. It became part of how we worked and celebrated each other.
- **Dual-purpose design** — One site served both the human needs of the team *and* the operational needs of the business. Built for two distinct audiences simultaneously.
